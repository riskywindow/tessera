### H9, stated at length because it decides the thesis

**Claim under test.** SM partitioning alone does not protect an interactive
decode tenant, because decode is memory-bound: a batch prefill tenant confined
to a small SM share can still saturate DRAM bandwidth and inflate decode TPOT.

**Method.** Confine the batch prefill tenant to k SMs via green contexts and
sweep k. For each k, report:

- the decode tenant's TPOT (p50 and p99), against its solo baseline;
- the batch tenant's SM share, k / 58 on the L4 measured in M0;
- NVML DRAM throughput, as the bandwidth-pressure signal.

**Why it is the headline.** If TPOT still degrades as k shrinks, that is the
finding the project is built on, and it is the reason the controller exists at
all: the interference signal has to include bandwidth, not just occupancy. If
TPOT does *not* degrade — if a small enough k protects decode — then SM
partitioning is sufficient, the bandwidth-aware controller is unnecessary, and
**the approved delta must be rewritten** rather than defended. H9 is therefore
allowed to falsify the project's own thesis, which is why it is hard.

**Dependencies.** H9 needs green contexts to work at all (H6) and needs the
preemption floor (H7) to interpret what remains after partitioning. It cannot
be run before H6 reports.
